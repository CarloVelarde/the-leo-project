## Constants we use

| Symbol | Meaning | Approx. value |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| $R_E$ | Earth mean radius | 6371 km |
| $\mu$ | Earth gravitational parameter | $3.986004418 \times 10^5$ km³/s² |
| $c$ | Speed of light | $2.99792458 \times 10^5$ km/s |

These match `src/sim/constants.ts` so lesson math and lab math stay honest.

## Circular orbit period

Semi-major axis for a circular orbit at altitude $h$:

$$
a = R_E + h
$$

Period (Kepler, circular):

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}}
$$

Orbital speed:

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a}}
$$

**Try it:** At $h = 550$ km, $a = 6921$ km, $T$ is roughly **95 minutes**, $v$ about **7.6 km/s**. Use the interactive below and the lab insight panel to confirm the same trends.

## Light-time

One-way vacuum propagation over distance $d$:

$$
t = \frac{d}{c}
$$

Round-trip on the same path ≈ $2d/c$. For a satellite nearly overhead, $d \approx h$ is a lower bound (real slant ranges are longer when elevation is low).

GEO check: $h \approx 35786$ km → one-way ≈ $119$ ms → up+down ≈ **238 ms**.

<Callout title="Model honesty" variant="warning">
The lab ignores atmospheric refraction, processing delay, and multi-hop routing. Numbers are geometric floors and Kepler periods — teaching tools, not RF link budgets.
</Callout>

## Footprint half-angle (geometry)

With Earth radius $R_E$, orbit radius $a$, and minimum elevation $\varepsilon$:

$$
\lambda = \arccos\!\left(\frac{R_E}{a}\cos\varepsilon\right) - \varepsilon
$$

Here $\lambda$ is the Earth-central angle from the sub-satellite point to the edge of geometric visibility. Higher $h$ (larger $a$) tends to enlarge the footprint; higher $\varepsilon$ shrinks usable coverage.

The 3D lab draws this footprint for the serving sat when the overlay is enabled.

## Why J2 / drag are omitted (for now)

Real LEO orbits precess and decay. Station-keeping fights that (core Module 4). For intuition about coverage density and latency trends, **circular Kepler** is the right first model — the same philosophy ESA’s qualitative orbit pages use before diving into perturbations (<InlineSource id="esaOrbits" />).

<Advanced title="Optional derivation sketch: period">
Newton + centripetal: $\frac{GM}{a^2} = \frac{v^2}{a}$ with $v = \frac{2\pi a}{T}$ yields $T^2 \propto a^3$ — Kepler’s third law for circular orbits. $\mu = GM$ packs the constants.
