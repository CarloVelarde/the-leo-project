# Optional: Direct-to-cell and future evolution

The core path focused on **broadband user terminals** (flat phased-array dishes). Starlink and others also pursue **direct-to-cell (DTC)**: connecting ordinary phones to satellites for messaging and eventually broader service. This track situates DTC in the same architecture language you already have.

## Two different last hops

| | Dish broadband | Direct-to-cell |
|--|----------------|----------------|
| User hardware | Dedicated terminal | Ordinary phone (partner networks) |
| Antenna gain | High (phased array panel) | Low (phone antenna) |
| Typical use | Home/business broadband | Emergency/messaging → expanding |
| Link budget | Designed for high rate | Extremely constrained |

Same constellation ideas (shells, planes, handovers) — **different RF and product constraints**.

## Why lower shells show up in DTC plans

Public Starlink constellation altitude documentation associates **direct-to-cell** variants with **lower altitude bands** (on the order of ~330–370 km in 2026 planning tables), while broadband shells occupy nearby but distinct bands during shell-lowering campaigns (<InlineSource id="starlinkAltitudes" />).

Lower altitude can help the brutal **link budget** to a low-gain phone antenna (stronger signals, shorter range) — at the cost of more drag, more station-keeping, and different coverage geometry.

## Spectrum and partnerships

DTC is not only a space problem. It depends on:

- licensed spectrum and regulator approvals,  
- partnerships with mobile network operators,  
- protocols that tolerate high path loss and mobility,  
- and realistic service expectations (start with SMS-class, grow carefully).

Official technology pages frame the broader LEO product family; treat DTC timelines as evolving (<InlineSource id="starlinkTech" />).

## Future evolution (what to watch)

1. **Shell lowering / re-shelling** — safety and capacity driven.  
2. **More optical mesh capability** — long-haul in space.  
3. **Terminal generations** — performance, cost, power.  
4. **DTC feature expansion** — rates, roaming, regions.  
5. **Competition** — other LEO systems change the design environment.

<Callout title="Stay calibrated" variant="note">
When you read a press release, translate it into: which shell, which user antenna class, which link (user/gateway/ISL), and what independent tracking shows is actually on orbit.
